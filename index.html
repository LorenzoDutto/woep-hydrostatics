Equations used

Heave equilibrium (vertical force balance, the condition used above to find D): the buoyancy equals the total weight, ρw g Vs = Mtot g. The submerged volume Vs is a known function of the draft through the hull dimensions, while Mtot does not depend on D: the draft is the value of D that makes the two sides equal.

Steel: ts is an equivalent thickness: it spreads plates, stiffeners and bulkheads over the outer shell, so it is larger than the plate thickness. ρsteel = 7850 kg/m³.

Turbine: Mturbine = MRNA + Mtower. The RNA mass does not depend on the tower; its CoM is at hub height. The tower mass grows linearly with the hub height ht above the tower base (fixed rotor, NREL cost and scaling model, Fingersh et al. 2006): Mtower = 1040 t · ht / 135 m, with its CoM at 0.40 ht. For ht = 135 m the turbine weighs 2 100 t with its CoM 95 m above the tower base.

CoM of the whole device (platform + ballast + turbine): zCoM = Σ mi zi / Mtot, xCoM = Σ mi xi / Mtot. CoB = centroid of the submerged volume (only the hull contributes).

Reference frame. All positions are measured in a frame fixed to the structure, with the origin on the platform axis at the still water line (SWL) of the upright floating position, x downwind and z upward. All the hydrostatic quantities (zCoB, zCoM, J, K) are computed in the upright position. In the figures and in the frames table, the pitched platform is shown rotated by θ about this origin. The 1D model has a single degree of freedom (pitch), so translations of the platform (surge, heave) are not represented.

CoB when the platform pitches. The CoM is fixed to the structure and simply rotates with it. The CoB is not: the immersed side gains volume and the emerged side loses it, so the CoB moves towards the immersed side. For small angles, the buoyancy always acts along a vertical line through the metacentre M, which is fixed to the structure at zM = zCoB + BM, with BM = J / Vs. The horizontal distance between CoB and CoM is the lever arm of the righting couple of weight and buoyancy.

Pitch restoring stiffness K. In the general 6-DOF description, the restoring forces of a floating body are given by a 6 × 6 stiffness matrix C (hydrostatic + gravity, plus mooring). The 1D approach keeps only pitch, the rotation about the y axis through the origin (DOF 5), so C reduces to its pitch–pitch term:
K = C55 = ρw g (J + Vs zCoB) − Mtot g zCoM = ρw g Vs · GM,  with GM = zM − zCoM
The couplings with the other DOFs and the mooring stiffness are neglected. K gives the restoring moment for a small pitch angle: Mrestoring = K θ.

Static pitch: moment balance about the origin, K θ = Mext, with the overturning moment
Mext = FT hhub + Mtot g (xCoM − xCoB)  ⇒  θ = Mext / K
The first term is the moment of the thrust, applied at the hub height above the SWL. The second term is the couple of weight and buoyancy (equal in size at heave equilibrium) when CoM and CoB are not on the same vertical in the upright position. For these symmetric hulls xCoB = 0, and xCoM < 0 because of the overhanging rotor.

Blade tip clearance: hhub − R (upright) and −xhub sin θ + (hhub − R) cos θ (pitched), with R = 121 m; the smaller value must be ≥ the minimum clearance.

Failure checks: no room for the ballast (hb larger than the space available); sinking (Mtot > ρw Vs of the fully submerged hull); unstable (K ≤ 0); capsize (|θ| larger than the angle at which the hull top goes under water or the keel comes out of the water); blade tip clearance below the minimum.
