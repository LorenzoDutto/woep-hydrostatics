Conventions

(S2) M = r × F

Reference frame

Origin O at the still water level (SWL) on the platform axis, x downwind (direction of the wind and of the thrust), z up, y completing a right-handed frame. All positions are measured in a frame fixed to the structure, in the upright floating position.

Positive pitch ξ5 is a rotation about +y that tilts the tower top downwind (towards +x).

The model is linear (small angles), hydrostatic only (no waves, no dynamics) and has no mooring. It keeps one degree of freedom, pitch: surge and heave are not represented while the platform pitches.

Sign of a pitch moment. With M = r × F (S2), the pitch moment about O is My = z Fx − x Fz, where (x, z) is the point where the force F = (Fx, 0, Fz) is applied. My > 0 tilts the tower top downwind.

Equations used

Labels in brackets (S1, S7, …) are the equation numbers of the lecture slides.

Frame. Origin O at the still water level on the platform axis, x downwind, z up, y completing a right-handed frame. Positive pitch ξ5 tilts the tower top downwind. Small angles, linear hydrostatics, no mooring. All the hydrostatic quantities (CoB0Z, CoMZ, J, K55) are computed in the upright position; in the figures and in the positions table, the pitched platform is shown rotated by ξ5 about O. The model has a single degree of freedom (pitch), so surge and heave of the pitched platform are not represented.

Heave equilibrium (S1) (vertical force balance, the condition used above to find D): the buoyancy equals the total weight, ρw g Vs = m g. The submerged volume Vs is a known function of the draft through the hull dimensions, while m does not depend on D: the draft is the value of D that makes the two sides equal. The heave stiffness is K33 = ρw g Awp (S13).

Steel: ts is an equivalent thickness: it spreads plates, stiffeners and bulkheads over the outer shell, so it is larger than the plate thickness. ρsteel = 7850 kg/m³.

Turbine: Mturbine = MRNA + Mtower. The RNA mass does not depend on the tower; its CoM is at hub height. The tower mass grows linearly with the hub height ht above the tower base (fixed rotor, NREL cost and scaling model, Fingersh et al. 2006): Mtower = 1040 t · ht / 135 m, with its CoM at 0.40 ht. For ht = 135 m the turbine weighs 2 100 t with its CoM 95 m above the tower base.

CoM of the whole device (platform + ballast + turbine): CoMZ = Σ mi zi / m, CoMX = Σ mi xi / m. CoB = centroid of the submerged volume (only the hull contributes).

CoB when the platform pitches. The CoM is fixed to the structure and simply rotates with it. The CoB is not: the immersed side gains volume and the emerged side loses it, so the CoB moves towards the immersed side. For small angles, the buoyancy always acts along a vertical line through the metacentre M, which is fixed to the structure at zM = CoB0Z + BM, with BM = J / Vs. The horizontal distance CoBX(ξ5) − CoMX(ξ5) is the lever arm of the righting couple of weight and buoyancy.

Pitch stiffness K55 (S12). In the general 6-DOF description, the restoring forces of a floating body are given by a 6 × 6 stiffness matrix K (hydrostatic + gravity, plus mooring). The 1D approach keeps only pitch, the rotation about the y axis through O (DOF 5), so K reduces to its pitch–pitch term:
K55 = ρw g J + ρw g Vs CoB0Z − m g CoMZ = ρw g Vs · GM  (S12),  with GM = zM − CoMZ
CoB0Z < 0 always, so the middle term always destabilises. The couplings with the other DOFs and the mooring stiffness are neglected. K55 gives the restoring moment for a small pitch angle: Mrestoring = K55 ξ5.

Static pitch (S10): moment balance about O (S7), K55 ξ5 = Mext, with the overturning moment
Mext = FT hhub + m g CoMX − ρw g Vs CoB0X  (S3 + S4 + S5 at ξ5 = 0)  ⇒  ξ5 = Mext / K55  (S10)
The first term is the moment of the thrust, applied at the hub height above the SWL. The other two are the couple of weight and buoyancy (equal in size at heave equilibrium, m = ρw Vs) when CoM and CoB are not on the same vertical in the upright position. For these symmetric hulls CoB0X = 0, and CoMX < 0 because of the overhanging rotor. Neglecting the couple gives S11: ξ5 = FT hhub / K55.

Blade tip clearance: hhub − R (upright) and −xhub sin ξ5 + (hhub − R) cos ξ5 (pitched), with R = 121 m; the smaller value must be ≥ the minimum clearance.

Failure checks: no room for the ballast (hb larger than the space available); sinking (m > ρw Vs of the fully submerged hull); unstable (K55 ≤ 0); limit of the linear model (|ξ5| larger than the angle at which the deck edge goes under water or the keel comes out of the water); blade tip clearance below the minimum.
